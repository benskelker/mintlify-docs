This topic describes the three general methodologies for operating models, also referred to as service delivery models, when it comes to identity security programs: top-down, bottom-up, or a hybrid approach. Each of these has its own benefits and trade-offs.

Top-down approach
-------------------------------

A top-down approach to service delivery is mandate-driven with an [enforceable security policy](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/enterprise-security-policy), driven through the office of the CISO or CIO. This approach must be in alignment with and supported by various LoB Security and Business Leaders. This approach ensures collective risk mitigation across the organization versus delivering pockets of risk “mitigation” (mitigation is in quotes to reference the fact that only partially securing access to a resource or environment means that it’s still unprotected). The security organization owns the responsibility for working with various stakeholders to ensure they are protected, which enables a proactive approach to security and one that can be enforced through automation.

Bottom-up approach
--------------------------------

A bottom-up approach to service delivery is a self-service approach to security, whereby instead of having enforceable security policy and top-down alignment, the ownership to execute is put on individuals within lines of business (whether for an identity, account, or system owner). This is more in line with a service offering, but there’s no requirement to use it – the users and Lines of Business can opt in or out. Bottom-up models are typically implemented as a request-based system for onboarding and securing privileged access, which leads to a reactive approach to security.

Hybrid approach
-----------------------------

Organizations may opt for a hybrid operating model, where they pick and choose elements of both models. A hybrid approach may enable you to get the benefits of the top-down approach, but perhaps put the ownership for execution on the various lines of businesses or divisions. You give them the mandate and the tools for the job, but it’s up to them to own the responsibility for execution. This typically requires heavy amounts of automation and predefined request system to ensure success.

Organizations striving for a program mindset leverage the top-down or hybrid model for long-term identity security success. Organizations which leverage a bottom-up “field of dreams” approach to identity security may find short-term success, but they often fail to mitigate risk in a strategic and meaningful way across the enterprise. That approach often struggles to follow the [operationalization guidance](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/operationalize-identity-security) in the next section, which leads to an ever-increasing cybersecurity debt gap. If you find yourself feeling like you’re in a bottom-up service delivery model, leverage the information from this book to build the business case for better buy-in and create that top-down approach.
