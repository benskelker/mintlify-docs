This topic explains the importance of enterprise security policies in protecting an organization’s assets, data, applications, and infrastructure.

Enterprise or corporate security policies are a set of rules that an organization defines, documents, and then implements controls and processes to adhere to them. These security policies are written to protect an organization’s assets, data, applications, and infrastructure. These policies are often derived from cybersecurity frameworks, regulatory standards ,and other security best practices to ensure that the organization stays secure and complies with any legal and regulatory standards. Within the realm of identity security, organizations often define security policy around the use of MFA, protection of privileged access, usage of administrative permissions, creation of accounts, governance of identity, password policy, and many other topics.

Having documented security policies that are signed off by security and business leaders provides security teams with an enforceable mandate to hold various business units accountable for compliance with their corporate define policies, and this can be used to force the remediation of security risks. These documented and agreed-upon policies are valuable to security teams as they’re often held accountable for someone else’s risk. Without them, they would have no mechanism to shift that responsibility back to the rightful owner of the risk.

Organizations without enterprise security policies for identity security, privilege, and access controls often operate with a "bottom-up approach." This approach involves convincing business units to protect themselves rather than assisting them in executing security controls, which is a less effective method for reducing risk across the organization. Inversely, organizations sometimes find themselves pigeon-holed with how they craft their security policy, making it so ridged that new technologies, solutions, and controls, while capable of mitigating the risk, are not considered acceptable because the language used in the policy is either dated or too specific. It’s recommended that as organizations undergo major strategic cybersecurity initiatives and planning that they re-evaluate their security policies to ensure they’re reflective of the current state of industry.
