Implementing an effective identity security program can be a complex and challenging endeavor, especially for organizations that lack well-defined processes for managing privileged access. This topic explains how to leverage process maturity models like the Capability Maturity Model Integration (CMMI) framework to establish a more effective identity security program.

It’s important to note that the Capability Maturity Model Integration (CMMI) is a framework for process improvement, and as such, it can be applied to any process within an organization, including identity security. CMMI provides general best practices for organizations to follow in order to improve their processes and increase their capability to deliver high-quality products and services. CMMI uses a structured approach to process improvement, with five maturity levels that represent progressively higher levels of process capability and maturity. Each maturity level includes a set of process areas, which are collections of related best practices that help organizations achieve specific goals for process improvement. The CMMI framework can be applied to a wide range of industries and processes, including software development, project management, systems engineering, and of course, cybersecurity operations.

There are several benefits in leveraging the CMMI model for identity security, including improved security, reduced risk, greater compliance, increased efficiency, and continuous improvement. By following a mature and well-defined process for managing privileged access, organizations can better protect their sensitive information and systems from malicious actors and unauthorized access, resulting in risk mitigation. This can lead to cost savings related to incident response, remediation, and recovery efforts. It provides you with a tangible model to mature your identity security program and processes over time, as opposed to trying to dive into the deep end immediately.

Below you’ll find an explanation for how each level of the CMMI might be applied to an identity security program and the resources that are helpful for you to accomplish this level of maturity.

Level 1: Initial
------------------------------

At this level, the identity security program may not have a defined process for managing privileged access. The program may rely on manual processes, spreadsheets, or other ad-hoc methods for managing privileged access. The program may also lack visibility into which users have privileged access and which actions they are taking. Privileged access and the management of it is unpredictable, poorly controlled, and reactive. Organizations should actively seek to move away from this level of maturity. For organizations at this level, we recommend becoming familiar with the CyberArk Blueprint, our best practices program framework to understand the importance of identity security and actively focus on rapid risk mitigation for critical risk use cases.

Level 2: Managed
------------------------------

At this level, the organization has established basic processes for managing privileged access. The organization may have implemented an identity security solution that provides centralized management and control over privileged access. The organization may also have defined roles and responsibilities for managing privileged access and may have established a process for discovering, onboarding, requesting, approving, and auditing privileged access. Privileged access and the management of it is often project-driven for a specific subset of privileged access, and is reactive, typically the result of an auditing finding, penetration test, or security breach. At this level, the organization is still operating identity security as a project, not a program. For organizations aspiring to obtain this level of maturity, we recommend reviewing the relevant [use case specific best practices](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/use-case-best-practices) that are in scope for your current phase of deployment. We recommend that you simultaneously formalize policies, standards, and procedures for privileged access enterprise-wide and begin building a plan for future privileged access work.

Level 3: Defined
------------------------------

At this level, the identity security initiative has evolved from project to program, and has documented its processes and procedures for managing privileged access. The program has established a standard process for discovering, onboarding, requesting, approving, and auditing privileged access. The program may also have defined metrics for measuring the effectiveness of its identity security controls and may have established a process for continuous improvement. The organization has established some level of enterprise-wide standards and policies for privileged access, including well defined definitions and policy exceptions. The organization has developed a privileged access management roadmap and has a long-term vision for how to address their identity security program. For organizations aspiring to obtain this level of maturity, we recommend reviewing our guidance on [assessing your security posture](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/assess-security-posture) and [building your roadmap](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/build-roadmap). Organizations at this level should look to formalize reporting processes to self-assess and reflect on the effectiveness of their program and identify areas of improvement.

Level 4: Quantitatively managed
---------------------------------------------

At this level, the identity security program uses data and metrics to measure the effectiveness of its identity security controls. The program may be using tools to monitor privileged access and detect anomalies or unauthorized activity. The program may also be conducting regular audits of privileged access and using the results to improve its identity security controls. The organization is able to quantify the scope of privilege and leverage metrics to establish reportable KPIs about the effectiveness of their identity security program and its controls. For organizations aspiring to obtain this level of maturity, we recommend reviewing our guidance on [measuring success and program reporting](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/measure-and-report-success).

Level 5: Optimized
--------------------------------

At this level, the identity security program is continuously improving its identity security controls based on data and feedback. The program may be using machine learning or other advanced technologies to detect and respond to privileged access risks. The program may also be collaborating with other teams within the organization to integrate identity security controls into other processes and systems. The main focus at this level is on process improvement, often done by boosting automation capabilities and privileged lifecycle management to streamline security operations. The organization continues to seek out new areas to apply privileged access management controls to (for example, coverage of technologies, personas, or use cases) or to mature the security controls being applied (for example, move from personal account to shared, move from freestanding to JIT, and so on). For organizations aspiring to obtain this level of maturity, we recommend reviewing our guidance on [operationalizing identity security](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/operationalize-identity-security), the [state of privilege and access hierarchy](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/state-of-privilege-access-hierarchy), and [navigating access in today’s environments](/cyberark-blueprint/latest/en/content/navigate-access-todays-env).
