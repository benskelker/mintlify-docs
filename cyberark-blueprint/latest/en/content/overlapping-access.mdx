This topic explores the concept of overlapping access to common resources and emphasizes the importance of holistic identity security to protect enterprise resources from malicious actors. This concept rounds out the picture of identity risk.

Part of why it’s so important to think about identity security holistically, from the perspective of all these different identities, is their overlapping access to common enterprise resources. Malicious actors know how to circumnavigate organizations and exploit those areas of overlapping access to their advantage. This overlapping access is what enables malicious actors to move laterally from resource to resource, and eventually move vertically based on the access they find. Protecting IT admins’ privileged access to virtual machines but leaving developers privileged access to those same VMs unprotected means that your VMs are not truly protected, and your IT admins may be at an increased risk for compromise as a result as well.

Holistic security encompasses all the identities which access enterprise resources, whether operational (personal), system (built-in local admins and break-glass accounts) or machine (non-human workload) access. You can’t call a specific enterprise resource secured until all the identities that access it are secured. Many PAM programs traditionally focused on protecting IT and machine access to VMs and infrastructure, but as you can see from all these overlapping rows and columns, malicious actors can navigate their way around and continue to wreak havoc with the overlapping access. Only focusing on one identity’s access to a specific resource type leaves additional avenues for malicious actors to compromise the resource and then circumnavigate your organization.
