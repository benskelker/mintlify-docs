This topic explains how to prioritize securing identities, access, and enterprise resources using CyberArk's "risk-effort index".

As organizations start to see the expansive scope of access for various identities, they quickly begin to realize that they need a method for prioritization of securing these identities, access, and enterprise resources. As good security practitioners, you want to focus on reducing risk. The way CyberArk looks at prioritizing use cases uses a “risk-effort index”, comparing two key factors: the amount of risk reduction versus how much effort is required to mitigate that risk for a given use case.

As mentioned earlier, risk can be defined in many ways to different organizations, but in this context, we’re referring to the sum of those three factors: level of privilege, scope of influence, and ease of compromise. These factors are what drive the impact of a compromised identity, are common to all organizations, and are widely exploited by malicious actors. The larger the impact, the larger the risk. The larger the risk, the larger the risk mitigation by implementing intelligent privilege controls.

The second factor, level of effort, refers to how difficult it is, inclusive of technical, political or social complexity, and the time it takes to implement a particular identity security use case. Scenarios where implementation requires little customization or those that have native integrations are less technically complex. Scenarios where the identity user group is typically more standoffish or resistant to change are more politically or socially complex to implement because they require a higher level of organizational change management. Not every organization can have a top-down mandated approach to identity security, so advice should default to the use cases that are typically the easiest, or easier, to implement.

This “risk versus effort” logic enables us to prioritize the identity security use cases that give you the best "risk bang" for your "effort buck". If you assume organizations only have so much time, effort, and resources to accomplish anything, you want them to get the maximum risk reduction for their effort (risk reduction ROI or shareholder value). The CyberArk Blueprint recommendations are primarily driven from this perspective, starting with the high priority tasks, or quick wins, that will net us the most value for relatively low effort. From there, you conceptually move to either of the medium priority buckets, finally ending on the low priority use cases.

The Blueprint’s recommendations are built on more than two decades of lessons learned across CyberArk’s global 8,500+ customer base, post-breach incident response, frontline remediation, red-team engagements and CyberArk Lab’s innovative researchers. As such, we’ve developed a prescriptive opinion on what identity security use cases move the needle the most for IT, developer, workforce and machine identities.
