This topic explains how to apply appropriate levels of intelligent privilege control based on the risk associated with different identities and their access to enterprise resources.

A key aspect of effectively applying the appropriate level of intelligent privilege control to a specific identity and enterprise resource is first understanding that privilege controls are not a one-size-fits-all model. This is because not all access is equal. Certain identities and their access tend to be higher risk or lower risk – and this is fairly consistent across most organizations. You don’t want to apply too restrictive controls against users for whom it’s not warranted. Inversely, you need to make sure that high-risk access is protected properly.

Generally speaking, as the level of risk increases for a particular identity’s access to a given resource, so should the level of control and protection through the appropriate intelligent privilege controls. This thought process aligns with the concept of minimum effective control – you must put the appropriate control in place in accordance with risk, but not overburden the users or the operations and administrative teams.

For example, an IT Administrator role such as a Site Reliability Engineer commonly has administrative privileges across the organization’s Cloud Service Provider accounts. This is an extremely high-risk access scenario and as such, higher levels of intelligent privilege controls should be applied. Inversely, a general member of the organization’s workforce and their individual personal access to the company’s HR system is not as risky to the organization, so applying the same level of intelligent privilege control to that identity as the IT Admin would not be considered the minimum effective control.
