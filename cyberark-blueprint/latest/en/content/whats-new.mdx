Updated CyberArk Blueprint guidance is released and announced on a varying cadence. New versions do not require customer action, and may be released without an announcement.

August 29, 2024
-----------------------------

### CyberArk online documentation now available

The CyberArk Blueprint is now a part of the CyberArk online documentation. Previously, Blueprint materials were spread across various locations on the Technical Community or Resource Center, or were not available outside of a discussion with a CyberArk resource. We’ve made the majority of the CyberArk Blueprint material 100% public and available on the Online Docs.

### Alignment of guidance to common secured identities

The CyberArk Blueprint’s use case best-practice guidance has always traditionally focused on level of privilege and enterprise resources, regardless of the identity consuming the privilege or accessing the resource. With this release of the Blueprint, our guidance now aligns to the four common types of secured identities: workforce, IT, developers, and machines.

### Incorporation of zero standing privilege best practice guidance, revisions to other security control families

As identity security controls evolve to meet the needs of new environments, organizations are finding it hard to break through the noise about which controls are considered “Privileged Access Management”. To make things easier to identify which use cases require the application of just-in-time access and entitlements (zero standing privilege controls) compared to vaulting, rotation and isolation (secure standing privilege controls), we’ve revised our security control families and use case names.

Privilege controls are a spectrum, and there is a difference in how you solve federated human access versus human access with built-in local admin and system accounts, but both need to be accounted for. There are now five control families as a part of the Blueprint: security-first access management, endpoint privilege security, zero standing privilege, secure standing privilege, and secrets management.

### Revisions to the identity attack chain and guiding principles

Environments, identities, and attack techniques change, but the attack path remains constant. To better align security efforts to the identities which require protection and to incorporate new environments and attack methods, we’ve updated the identity attack chain to encompass more than credential theft. The first guiding principle of the Blueprint has been updated to prevent identity compromise, which is inclusive of credential theft, but also accounts for newer identity attack methods such as MFA-bypass, IdP attacks and web application session cookie compromise.

### Introduction of risk effort index, removal of stages

Previously, the Blueprint leveraged the concept of “stages” to articulate the priority of identity security use cases, centered around a particular goal. However, organizations felt it was confusing as their priorities didn’t always align to CyberArk’s stage grouped recommendations. To provide improved clarity, we’ve removed the concept of stages and introduced the “risk-effort index”, which had always been the underlying logic for stages. The risk-effort index is a prioritization matrix concept comparing the risk impact of specific identity security use cases to their level of effort required to implement. This concept guides CyberArk’s risk-based recommendations moving forward.

### Additional program-level guidance

This release of the CyberArk Blueprint includes additional program-level guidance, designed to help organizations better develop and operationalize their identity security programs,. This includes better resources on alignment to cybersecurity frameworks and standards, development of security policies, organizational change management, and more.

### Other minor revisions

Other minor revisions have been made to the CyberArk Blueprint, including a visual rebranding and small modifications to some terminology.
